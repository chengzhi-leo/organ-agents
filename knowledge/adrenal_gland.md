# Adrenal Gland

## Local Dynamics

### Adrenal Medulla

The adrenal medulla releases catecholamines, primarily epinephrine and norepinephrine, in response to sympathetic stimulation.

Increased sympathetic activation generally increases catecholamine secretion.

Catecholamine secretion provides a rapid endocrine response during physiological stress, exercise, and other conditions requiring increased metabolic and cardiovascular activity.

### Cortisol Secretion

The adrenal cortex produces cortisol, primarily under regulation by ACTH.

Increased ACTH generally increases cortisol synthesis and secretion.

Cortisol influences carbohydrate, protein, and lipid metabolism and supports physiological responses to prolonged stress.

Cortisol synthesis depends on adrenal steroidogenic capacity and availability of cholesterol-derived substrate.

### Aldosterone Secretion

The adrenal cortex produces aldosterone.

Angiotensin II and increased extracellular potassium are major stimuli for aldosterone secretion.

ACTH can also influence aldosterone secretion, although it is not the primary regulator under ordinary physiological conditions.

Aldosterone secretion generally increases when angiotensin II signaling or circulating potassium increases.

### Adrenal Steroid Synthesis

Adrenal cortical hormones are synthesized from cholesterol.

Steroid hormones are produced as needed rather than stored in large preformed secretory pools.

Changes in trophic hormonal stimulation and local steroidogenic activity therefore alter adrenal hormone production and secretion.
