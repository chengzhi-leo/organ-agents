# Parathyroid

## Local Dynamics

### Calcium Sensing

Parathyroid chief cells continuously sense extracellular ionized calcium through calcium-sensing receptors.

A decrease in circulating ionized calcium increases parathyroid hormone synthesis and secretion.

An increase in circulating ionized calcium suppresses parathyroid hormone secretion.

This calcium-sensitive regulation provides the primary physiological control of parathyroid activity.

### Parathyroid Hormone Secretion

Parathyroid hormone is synthesized by parathyroid chief cells and released into the circulation.

Changes in extracellular calcium can rapidly alter secretion of stored hormone, while sustained changes can also modify hormone synthesis.

Parathyroid hormone secretion therefore increases during hypocalcemic conditions and decreases when calcium availability is restored.

### Phosphate and Vitamin-D Regulation

Changes in phosphate balance can influence parathyroid hormone secretion, particularly through their effects on calcium availability and mineral-regulatory signaling.

Calcitriol generally suppresses parathyroid hormone synthesis and secretion and contributes to feedback regulation of parathyroid activity.

### Magnesium Regulation

Magnesium can influence parathyroid hormone secretion.

Moderate reductions in magnesium can stimulate parathyroid activity, whereas severe magnesium deficiency can impair parathyroid hormone secretion.
