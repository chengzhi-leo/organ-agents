# Pituitary

## Local Dynamics

### Anterior Pituitary Regulation

The anterior pituitary contains specialized endocrine cell populations that synthesize and secrete distinct pituitary hormones.

Hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting hormones reach the anterior pituitary through the hypophyseal portal circulation and regulate hormone secretion.

Corticotropin-releasing hormone promotes adrenocorticotropic hormone secretion.

Thyrotropin-releasing hormone promotes thyroid-stimulating hormone secretion and can also stimulate prolactin release.

Gonadotropin-releasing hormone stimulates luteinizing hormone and follicle-stimulating hormone secretion.

Growth-hormone-releasing hormone stimulates growth hormone secretion, whereas somatostatin suppresses growth hormone secretion.

Hypothalamic dopamine inhibits prolactin secretion.

### Hormonal Feedback

Circulating hormones produced by downstream endocrine tissues provide feedback to the pituitary.

Cortisol suppresses adrenocorticotropic hormone secretion.

Thyroid hormones suppress thyroid-stimulating hormone secretion.

Sex steroids and gonadal peptide hormones modify luteinizing hormone and follicle-stimulating hormone secretion.

Insulin-like growth factor signaling can suppress growth hormone secretion.

These feedback mechanisms stabilize activity within hypothalamic–pituitary endocrine axes.

### Growth Hormone Secretion

Growth hormone secretion is regulated by the balance between growth-hormone-releasing hormone and somatostatin.

Growth hormone secretion is pulsatile and varies with sleep, nutritional state, exercise, and other physiological conditions.

### Prolactin Secretion

Prolactin secretion is under predominant inhibitory control by hypothalamic dopamine.

Reduced dopaminergic inhibition increases prolactin secretion.

Prolactin secretion can also increase in response to appropriate neuroendocrine and reproductive signals.

### Posterior Pituitary Hormone Release

The posterior pituitary stores antidiuretic hormone and oxytocin synthesized by hypothalamic neurons.

Action potentials arriving through hypothalamic axons trigger calcium-dependent release of these hormones from posterior pituitary nerve terminals.

Increased activity of ADH-producing hypothalamic neurons increases ADH release.

Increased activity of oxytocin-producing neurons increases oxytocin release.
