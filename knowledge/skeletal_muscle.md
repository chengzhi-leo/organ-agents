# Skeletal Muscle

## Local Dynamics

### Contraction and Energy Demand

Motor neural stimulation activates skeletal-muscle fibers and produces contraction.

Muscle contraction increases ATP demand. The required energy can be supplied by stored high-energy phosphates, glycolysis, and oxidative metabolism according to workload, substrate availability, and oxygen availability.

Increasing workload generally increases substrate utilization and oxygen consumption.

### Glucose and Glycogen Metabolism

Skeletal-muscle glucose uptake depends on glucose availability, perfusion, glucose-transporter availability, and local energy demand.

Insulin increases membrane recruitment of insulin-sensitive glucose transporters and promotes glucose uptake.

Muscle contraction can independently increase glucose-transporter recruitment and glucose uptake.

Glucose taken up by muscle can be oxidized for ATP production or stored as glycogen.

Insulin and abundant substrate favor glycogen synthesis, whereas increased workload and catecholamine signaling can promote glycogenolysis.

Muscle glycogen is primarily used locally as an energy reserve rather than released as free glucose into the circulation.

### Lipid and Ketone Metabolism

Circulating fatty acids can be taken up and oxidized for ATP production or stored in intramuscular lipid pools.

Fatty-acid oxidation becomes an important source of energy during sustained aerobic activity and other conditions favoring lipid utilization.

Ketone bodies can also be taken up and oxidized as metabolic substrates when available.

### Lactate Metabolism

When glycolytic ATP production increases relative to oxidative metabolism, lactate production can increase.

Skeletal muscle can also take up and oxidize circulating lactate.

Net lactate exchange therefore depends on workload, oxygen availability, substrate availability, and local metabolic state.

### Protein Metabolism

Amino acids can be taken up by skeletal muscle and incorporated into muscle protein.

Protein synthesis and protein breakdown depend on amino-acid availability, hormonal signaling, mechanical activity, and local energy state.

Insulin generally favors protein synthesis and reduces protein breakdown, particularly when amino acids are available.

### Potassium Handling

Skeletal muscle exchanges potassium with the extracellular fluid through membrane ion transport.

Insulin promotes potassium uptake into muscle cells.

Muscle electrical activity and contraction produce dynamic potassium fluxes across the muscle-cell membrane.

### Perfusion and Oxygen Dependence

Local perfusion determines delivery of oxygen, hormones, and metabolic substrates and removal of metabolic products.

When oxygen availability is sufficient, oxidative metabolism supports sustained ATP production.

When oxygen delivery or oxidative capacity is insufficient relative to energy demand, reliance on glycolytic metabolism increases.
