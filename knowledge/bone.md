# Bone

## Local Dynamics

### Bone Remodeling

Bone is continuously remodeled through coordinated activity of bone-forming osteoblasts and bone-resorbing osteoclasts.

When bone formation exceeds bone resorption, bone mass and mineral deposition increase.

When bone resorption exceeds bone formation, mineralized bone is broken down and calcium and phosphate can be released from bone.

The balance between formation and resorption depends on hormonal signals, mechanical loading, mineral availability, and local cellular signaling.

### Parathyroid Hormone Regulation

PTH modifies bone remodeling through signaling within the bone microenvironment.

Sustained increases in PTH generally promote osteoclast-mediated bone resorption indirectly through osteoblast-lineage cells.

Increased bone resorption mobilizes calcium and phosphate stored in the mineralized bone matrix.

The effect of PTH depends on the magnitude and temporal pattern of PTH exposure.

### Vitamin-D and Mineralization

Calcitriol supports calcium and phosphate availability required for normal bone mineralization.

Adequate calcium and phosphate are necessary for deposition of hydroxyapatite within newly formed bone matrix.

Insufficient mineral availability can reduce effective bone mineralization.

Calcitriol can also influence osteoblast and osteoclast activity as part of normal bone remodeling.

### Calcitonin Regulation

Calcitonin can inhibit osteoclast activity and reduce bone resorption.

This effect can reduce release of calcium and phosphate from mineralized bone.

Calcitonin plays a relatively limited role in normal adult calcium regulation compared with PTH and vitamin D.

### Mechanical Regulation

Mechanical loading promotes maintenance and formation of bone in regions exposed to repeated stress.

Reduced mechanical loading or prolonged immobilization can favor bone loss and increased resorption.

Bone therefore adapts its structure and remodeling activity to local mechanical demands.
