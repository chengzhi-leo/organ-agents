# Thyroid

## Local Dynamics

### Thyroid Hormone Synthesis

Thyroid follicular cells take up circulating iodide and use it to synthesize the thyroid hormones thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3).

Iodide is incorporated into thyroglobulin within thyroid follicles, where thyroid hormone precursors are formed and stored in the colloid.

Adequate iodide availability is required for normal thyroid hormone synthesis.

### TSH Regulation

TSH is the major physiological stimulus for thyroid follicular-cell activity.

Increased TSH promotes iodide uptake, thyroid hormone synthesis, processing of stored thyroglobulin, and release of T4 and T3.

Reduced TSH decreases thyroid hormone synthesis and secretion.

Sustained TSH stimulation can also increase thyroid follicular-cell activity and growth.

### Thyroid Hormone Release

Most hormone released by the thyroid is T4, with smaller amounts of T3.

Stored thyroid hormone can be mobilized from thyroglobulin and released into the circulation in response to TSH stimulation.

The amount of hormone released depends on TSH signaling, iodide availability, stored hormone, and thyroid synthetic capacity.

### Calcitonin Secretion

Parafollicular C cells within the thyroid secrete calcitonin.

Increased circulating calcium can stimulate calcitonin secretion.

Calcitonin can reduce osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, although its role in normal adult calcium regulation is relatively limited compared with PTH and vitamin D.
