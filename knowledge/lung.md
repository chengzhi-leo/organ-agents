# Lung

## Local Dynamics

### Ventilation

Air moves between the atmosphere and alveoli according to pressure gradients generated during breathing.

Alveolar ventilation depends on respiratory rate, tidal volume, airway resistance, and the fraction of ventilation reaching gas-exchanging regions.

Changes in airway resistance or lung mechanics can alter effective ventilation.

### Pulmonary Gas Exchange

Oxygen diffuses from alveolar gas into pulmonary capillary blood, whereas carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into the alveoli.

Gas exchange depends on alveolar ventilation, pulmonary perfusion, alveolar-capillary surface area, diffusion properties, and the partial-pressure gradients for oxygen and carbon dioxide.

Reduced ventilation, impaired diffusion, or mismatching between ventilation and perfusion can reduce gas-exchange efficiency.

### Ventilation–Perfusion Matching

Efficient gas exchange requires appropriate matching between regional ventilation and pulmonary blood flow.

Regions receiving ventilation but little perfusion contribute less effectively to gas exchange, whereas perfused regions receiving little ventilation can impair oxygen transfer.

Local changes in ventilation and perfusion therefore alter overall pulmonary gas-exchange efficiency.

### Pulmonary Vascular Regulation

Pulmonary vascular tone determines resistance to blood flow through the lungs.

Low alveolar oxygen can produce local pulmonary vasoconstriction, redirecting blood away from poorly ventilated regions.

Changes in pulmonary vascular tone alter pulmonary vascular resistance and the distribution of pulmonary blood flow.

### Lung Mechanics

Lung expansion depends on the balance among transpulmonary pressure, elastic recoil, airway resistance, and surface tension.

Pulmonary surfactant reduces alveolar surface tension and increases the stability and compliance of alveoli.

Reduced lung compliance or increased airway resistance increases the mechanical work required for ventilation.

### Carbon-Dioxide and Acid–Base Regulation

Carbon dioxide generated by metabolism is transferred from pulmonary blood into alveolar gas and eliminated through ventilation.

Increased alveolar ventilation generally increases carbon-dioxide elimination, whereas reduced ventilation decreases carbon-dioxide elimination.

Through control of carbon-dioxide removal, pulmonary ventilation contributes to regulation of systemic acid–base balance.

### Metabolic Functions

Pulmonary endothelial and other lung cells can metabolize or modify selected circulating signaling molecules.

Angiotensin-converting enzyme in the pulmonary vascular endothelium contributes to conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II and degradation of several vasoactive peptides.
