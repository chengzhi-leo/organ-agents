# Skeletal Muscle

## State

Skeletal muscle is a major site of glucose uptake and glucose storage.

Local states relevant to glucose regulation include:

* net glucose uptake from blood
* insulin receptor activity
* insulin-sensitive glucose transporter activity and membrane availability
* intracellular glucose transport rate
* glucose phosphorylation
* muscle glycogen store
* glycogenesis
* glycogenolysis
* glucose utilization through glycolysis
* glycogen synthase activity
* glycogen phosphorylase activity

Skeletal muscle can store substantial glucose as glycogen.

Unlike the liver, muscle glycogen is primarily a local energy reserve. Skeletal muscle does not normally return its glycogen-derived glucose directly to the systemic circulation as free glucose.

## Inputs

### Insulin

Circulating insulin reaches skeletal muscle through the tissue blood supply and binds insulin receptors on muscle cells.

Insulin receptor activation controls recruitment of insulin-sensitive glucose transporters to the muscle-cell membrane.

### Circulating glucose

Glucose reaches skeletal muscle from the extracellular fluid and blood.

Its entry into muscle is strongly constrained by membrane glucose transport. Therefore increased extracellular glucose alone is not equivalent to unrestricted intracellular glucose availability.

### Muscle activity

Changes in muscle metabolic activity alter local glucose utilization and glycogen use.

Muscle contraction can therefore change the demand for glucose independently of changes originating in the pancreas or liver.

## Dynamics

An increase in circulating insulin increases insulin receptor activity on skeletal muscle cells.

Insulin receptor signaling rapidly increases the availability of insulin-sensitive glucose transporters at the cell membrane. This increases facilitated diffusion of glucose from extracellular fluid into muscle cells.

Once glucose enters the cell, phosphorylation traps it intracellularly and allows it to enter metabolic pathways.

When energy demand is low and insulin is high, glucose is preferentially stored as glycogen through increased glycogen synthesis.

When muscle metabolic demand increases, glucose utilization and glycogen breakdown can increase to supply ATP.

Because skeletal muscle does not normally export significant free glucose derived from its glycogen store, glycogen breakdown mainly supports local muscle metabolism rather than directly increasing blood glucose.

The acute effect of insulin on membrane glucose transport occurs faster than slower changes in glycogen stores and longer-term metabolic adaptation.

## Outputs

The principal whole-body observable output of skeletal muscle in the glucose-regulation model is:

* net glucose uptake from the circulation

Changes in insulin receptor activity, glucose transporter activity, intracellular glucose concentration, glycolysis, and glycogen storage are internal muscle states.

They affect the rest of the body primarily by changing how much circulating glucose skeletal muscle removes.
