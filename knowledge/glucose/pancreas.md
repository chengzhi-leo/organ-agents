# Pancreas

## State

The endocrine pancreas contains pancreatic islets with beta cells that synthesize, store, and secrete insulin and alpha cells that secrete glucagon.

Local states relevant to glucose regulation include:

* insulin synthesis and storage in beta-cell secretory granules
* insulin secretion rate
* glucagon secretion rate
* beta-cell glucose metabolism
* ATP-sensitive potassium channel activity
* beta-cell membrane potential
* voltage-gated calcium channel activity
* intracellular calcium concentration
* secretory granule exocytosis

These states and molecular activities belong to the pancreas itself.

## Inputs

### Circulating glucose

Blood glucose reaches the pancreatic islets through their rich blood supply.

Beta cells sense glucose through cellular glucose uptake and intracellular glucose metabolism. The resulting change in cellular energy state couples extracellular glucose concentration to insulin secretion.

Alpha-cell glucagon secretion is also sensitive to circulating glucose concentration.

### Amino acids

Increased circulating amino acids can stimulate insulin secretion and glucagon secretion.

### Gastrointestinal hormones

Hormonal signals released from the gastrointestinal tract after nutrient ingestion can augment insulin secretion.

### Autonomic input

Autonomic nervous activity can modify pancreatic hormone secretion.

## Dynamics

An increase in circulating glucose increases glucose metabolism inside beta cells.

Increased glucose metabolism raises the intracellular ATP-to-ADP ratio. Increased ATP closes ATP-sensitive potassium channels, reducing potassium efflux and depolarizing the beta-cell membrane.

Membrane depolarization opens voltage-gated calcium channels. Calcium enters the beta cell, increasing intracellular calcium and triggering exocytosis of insulin-containing secretory granules.

The rapid response is release of stored insulin. Continued glucose stimulation also increases insulin synthesis and replenishment of insulin stores on a slower timescale.

When circulating glucose decreases, beta-cell stimulation falls and insulin secretion decreases.

Glucagon secretion changes in the opposite direction to glucose under ordinary glucose-regulatory conditions: increased blood glucose suppresses glucagon secretion, whereas decreased blood glucose favors glucagon secretion.

Amino acids can stimulate both insulin and glucagon secretion, allowing protein ingestion to increase insulin without producing excessive hypoglycemia.

## Outputs

The endocrine pancreas releases hormones into the circulation.

Observable outputs are:

* insulin secretion into the blood
* glucagon secretion into the blood

The pancreas does not determine what insulin or glucagon subsequently do in liver, muscle, or other target tissues.
