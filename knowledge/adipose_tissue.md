# Adipose Tissue

## Local Dynamics

### Triglyceride Storage

Adipocytes store energy primarily as triglycerides.

Circulating fatty acids can be taken up and esterified into triglycerides for storage.

Fatty acids released from circulating triglyceride-rich lipoproteins can also be taken up by adipose tissue and incorporated into local triglyceride stores.

When energy and substrate availability are high, triglyceride storage increases.

### Glucose Metabolism and Lipogenesis

Insulin increases glucose uptake into adipocytes through insulin-sensitive glucose transport.

Glucose provides energy and metabolic intermediates that support triglyceride synthesis.

When carbohydrate and energy availability are high, adipose tissue can convert carbohydrate-derived substrates into fatty acids and triglycerides.

Insulin therefore generally promotes energy storage in adipose tissue.

### Lipolysis

Stored triglycerides can be hydrolyzed into fatty acids and glycerol through lipolysis.

Catecholamine signaling promotes lipolysis and mobilization of stored triglycerides.

Insulin suppresses lipolysis and favors triglyceride storage.

When lipolysis increases, free fatty acids and glycerol can be released from adipose tissue.

The balance between triglyceride synthesis and lipolysis determines the size of local lipid stores.

### Fatty-Acid Metabolism

Fatty acids taken up by adipocytes can be stored as triglycerides or used locally as metabolic substrates.

During conditions favoring energy mobilization, stored triglycerides are broken down and fatty acids become available for release or local oxidation.

Net fatty-acid uptake or release depends on substrate availability, hormonal signaling, and local energy state.

### Endocrine Function

Adipose tissue secretes signaling molecules that communicate nutritional and energy-storage status.

Leptin secretion generally reflects adipose energy stores and nutritional state.

Adipose tissue also produces adiponectin and other adipokines that influence metabolic regulation and insulin sensitivity.

The production of adipokines can change with adipocyte energy stores, nutritional state, and local tissue conditions.

### Perfusion and Oxygen Dependence

Local perfusion determines delivery of glucose, fatty acids, hormones, and oxygen to adipose tissue and removal of locally released metabolites.

Reduced perfusion or oxygen availability can limit substrate exchange and alter local adipocyte metabolism.
