# Endocrine Pancreas

## Local Dynamics

### Insulin Secretion

Pancreatic beta cells sense circulating glucose through glucose uptake and intracellular metabolism.

Increased glucose metabolism raises the intracellular ATP-to-ADP ratio, closes ATP-sensitive potassium channels, depolarizes the beta-cell membrane, increases calcium entry, and promotes exocytosis of insulin-containing granules.

Insulin secretion generally increases as glucose availability rises and decreases as glucose stimulation falls.

Incretin hormones amplify glucose-stimulated insulin secretion.

Amino acids and other nutrient-related signals can also stimulate or modulate insulin secretion.

Sustained stimulation can increase insulin synthesis and replenish secretory stores.

### Glucagon Secretion

Pancreatic alpha cells secrete glucagon in response to metabolic and neural signals.

Reduced glucose availability generally favors glucagon secretion, whereas elevated glucose suppresses glucagon secretion under ordinary glucose-regulatory conditions.

Amino acids can stimulate glucagon secretion, including under conditions in which insulin secretion is also increased.

Autonomic neural input and local intra-islet signals can further modify glucagon secretion.

### Somatostatin and Intra-Islet Regulation

Pancreatic delta cells secrete somatostatin in response to nutrient and local signals.

Somatostatin inhibits secretion from neighboring alpha and beta cells and contributes to local regulation of islet endocrine activity.

Insulin, glucagon, and other locally released signals can also influence neighboring islet cells through paracrine interactions.

### Neural Regulation

Autonomic neural signals can rapidly modify endocrine pancreatic secretion.

The direction and magnitude of the response depend on the receptor pathways activated and the endocrine cell type involved.
